Macro Mispricing Radar

Forecasters tell markets how each economy will do, and markets price that in. When an economy then delivers less but its stock market does not come down, the market has not caught up with reality. This page finds those markets, and the reverse, so an asset manager knows where to look first for short and long ideas.

Method, sources and limits
  • Forecasts. IMF World Economic Outlook, every release from April 2008 to April 2026 (GDP growth, inflation, government balance). OECD Economic Outlook, November 2023 to June 2026 (adds short and long interest rates). World Bank Global Economic Prospects, June 2024 and June 2026. European Commission (AMECO forecast archive), 28 releases from 2011 to November 2025, for the 16 markets it covers (the US, Japan, Korea, Australia, Canada, Mexico, Turkey, Switzerland and EU members). The OECD, World Bank and EC do not forecast Singapore or Hong Kong, so those two rely on the IMF.
  • What happened. The first figure the IMF published after each year ended, so later revisions do not rewrite history. 2020 and 2021 are left out of forecast errors because no one could foresee the pandemic.
  • Correcting forecasts. Each forecaster's next-year number is adjusted by its own average past error for that country, trusted in proportion to how much history there is (n ÷ (n + 4)). The IMF has about 15 years per country, the European Commission about 9, the OECD 2 and the World Bank 1, so the last two get small corrections.
  • Market. World Bank / S&P equity index annual change in USD against IMF nominal GDP in USD; and World Bank market cap as % of GDP against its own 10-year median. Market cap / GDP points that jump over 50% in a year while the index moved under 25% are treated as data breaks and skipped: .
  • Scoring. Each signal is ranked against the other markets (z-score). A market's score is the average of the signals you switch on. Roughly the top and bottom fifth are called over- and under-priced.
  • Track record. The same calculation at every April since 2012, using only what was known then, held for 3 years (or 1 year for the next-year view), USD index returns.
  • Not a buy or sell list. It is the economy step of a top-down process. The track record shows when the rule worked and when it did not.
